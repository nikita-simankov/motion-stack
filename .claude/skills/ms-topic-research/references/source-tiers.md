# Source Tiers

## Tier 1 — Primary and authoritative

Use when a claim must be unassailable in front of a technical or executive audience.

Examples:

- Peer-reviewed papers in named journals
- Government statistical agencies (BLS, Eurostat, ONS, etc.)
- Official product documentation from the vendor
- Official company filings, earnings calls, published roadmaps
- Direct interviews conducted by the studio or a tier-1 outlet
- Standards bodies (W3C, IETF, ISO, IEEE)
- Court filings and regulatory decisions

Rules:

- Always prefer the most recent edition
- Always check whether the source has been superseded
- Always record the access date

## Tier 2 — Reputable secondary

Use when primary is unavailable but the claim is well-established.

Examples:

- Major newspapers with editorial standards (NYT, FT, WSJ, Le Monde, etc.)
- Named industry publications with editorial staff (The Information, Ars Technica, Stratechery)
- Analyst reports from recognized firms (Gartner, Forrester, IDC) with disclosed methodology
- Technical conference talks (QCon, Strange Loop, PyCon, etc.)
- Widely cited books with named authors and publishers

Rules:

- Prefer pieces that link to their primary sources
- Downgrade opinion pieces with no sourcing
- Record the publication date and whether the piece has been corrected

## Tier 3 — Useful but soft

Use for color, not for load-bearing claims.

Examples:

- Well-trafficked blogs with a named author and a track record
- Documentation from adjacent or competing projects
- Community consensus with visible evidence (threads, issues, RFCs)
- Industry surveys with disclosed methodology but small samples

Rules:

- Never the sole source for a claim
- Always marked soft in the dossier
- Never used in the first two seconds of a script

## Rejected

Never cite.

- Anonymous posts
- Unattributed claims
- SEO content farms
- AI-generated content sites without named editors
- Undated articles on aging topics
- Marketing pages that misstate or hide their own sources
- Social posts without a link to a primary source
- Aggregators that strip attribution

## Edge cases

- Vendor blog with a named engineer and code samples: tier 3, upgradeable to tier 2 if peer-reviewed elsewhere
- Wikipedia: not a source, but a source-finder. Chase the footnotes.
- Press releases: not a source. Chase the underlying filing or paper.
- Preprints (arXiv, SSRN): tier 2 if widely cited, tier 3 if not.
- Tweets from named researchers: tier 3, upgradeable to tier 2 if the thread links to their own paper.
