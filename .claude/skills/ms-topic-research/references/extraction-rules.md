# Extraction Rules

How to pull claims out of a source without distorting them.

## One claim per falsifiable sentence

A claim is a statement that could be checked and found false. "The API is popular" is not a claim. "The API was called 4.2 billion times in Q3 2024" is a claim.

## Preserve scope

If the source says "in the US", do not write "globally". If the source says "among enterprises over 1,000 employees", do not write "among enterprises". Scope loss is the most common form of distortion.

## Preserve date

Every claim carries the date of the source. A claim from 2019 about market share is not a claim about today.

## Preserve confidence words

If the source says "estimated", "projected", "preliminary", keep those words. Do not promote an estimate to a fact.

## Preserve caveats

If the source says "excluding China" or "on a same-store basis", keep the caveat. Drop only caveats that are irrelevant to the brief.

## Do not chain

If source A cites source B, chase B. Do not cite A citing B unless A adds analysis. Never cite a citation.

## Do not synthesize across sources

Two sources saying similar things do not become one stronger claim. Record two claims, or record one and note the corroboration separately.

## Do not extrapolate

If a source says "X grew 20% year over year", do not write "X will grow 20% next year". Trends are not forecasts unless the source forecasts.

## Do not editorialize

Strip adjectives that come from the writer, not the source. "Devastating growth" is not a claim, it's a framing.

## Flag borderline

When unsure whether a sentence is a claim or an opinion, put it in the unused section with the reason. Better unused than wrong.
