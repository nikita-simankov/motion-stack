# Concept Frameworks

Structural families. Each concept picks exactly one. Two concepts in the same family is one concept.

## Claim-first

Structure: state the thesis in the first two seconds. Defend for the body. Land the CTA.
Best for: authority objectives, practitioner audiences, 30-60s.
Hook example: "Most teams deploy MCP wrong. Here's the fix."
Beat map: hook (thesis) → why it's true → three proofs → objection → CTA.
Risk: medium. Fails when the thesis is obvious.

## Question-first

Structure: open with the question the audience is already asking. Answer in stages.
Best for: consideration objectives, mixed audiences, 30-60s.
Hook example: "Why does your agent forget everything between calls?"
Beat map: question → why it matters → false answer → true answer → implication → CTA.
Risk: low. Fails when the question is boring or already answered.

## Contrast

Structure: before/after, old way/new way, us/them. Two states held side by side.
Best for: conversion objectives, decision-maker audiences, 15-30s.
Hook example: "This is how you do it. This is how everyone else does it."
Beat map: state A → state B → why B wins → proof → CTA.
Risk: low. Fails when the contrast is cosmetic.

## Demonstration

Structure: show the thing working. Narrate what it means.
Best for: consideration objectives, UI mockup heavy, 30-60s.
Hook example: "Watch what happens when I click this."
Beat map: setup → action → visible result → why it matters → CTA.
Risk: medium. Fails when the demo is slow or the result is unclear.

## Story

Structure: a specific person, moment, or decision with a resolution.
Best for: authority and awareness objectives, 60-90s.
Hook example: "In 2023, a team of four shipped what a team of forty couldn't."
Beat map: setup → tension → decision → outcome → meaning → CTA.
Risk: high. Fails when the story is generic or the resolution is weak.

## List

Structure: N reasons, N mistakes, N signals. Ordered for escalation.
Best for: awareness and consideration objectives, 30-60s.
Hook example: "Three signs your pipeline is about to break."
Beat map: count promise → item 1 → item 2 → item 3 → wrap → CTA.
Risk: low. Fails when the items are not distinct or not ordered.

## Absurd escalation

Structure: take the premise seriously to its logical extreme. Land the point on the way back.
Best for: awareness objectives, punchy tone, 15-30s.
Hook example: "If you replace every service with an agent, what breaks first?"
Beat map: premise → escalation 1 → escalation 2 → breaking point → landing → CTA.
Risk: high. Fails when the landing is not earned.

## Counterintuitive

Structure: lead with the claim the audience will resist. Then land it with evidence.
Best for: authority objectives, practitioner audiences, 30-60s.
Hook example: "Stop writing tests. Here's what to do instead."
Beat map: provocation → why it sounds wrong → why it's right → proof → caveat → CTA.
Risk: high. Fails when the provocation is clickbait with no substance.

## Framework selection rules

- Match the framework to the objective: awareness → story, absurd escalation, list. Consideration → demonstration, question-first, list. Conversion → contrast, demonstration. Authority → claim-first, counterintuitive, story.
- Match to runtime: 15s supports contrast, list (2 items), absurd escalation. 30s supports most. 60s+ supports story, demonstration with multiple steps.
- Match to concept posture: safe → claim-first, contrast, list. Distinct → demonstration, question-first, claim-first. Bold → counterintuitive, absurd escalation, story. Experimental → any, with a willingness to kill.
- Never pick two concepts from the same family in one run.
